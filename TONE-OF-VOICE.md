## 1. Tone of Voice

MindCanopy sounds like the friend who listens before they answer. Not a clinician, not a coach. Someone who takes you at your word, and takes their time with what you've said.

### The Feeling We Want to Leave

When someone reads a MindCanopy page, they should feel understood before they feel informed. They should feel like they've just spoken to someone who genuinely cares about what they're going through — not someone who's trying to sign them up for something.

The brand never performs confidence. It never draws attention to itself. Every sentence is in service of the person reading it, not of making MindCanopy sound impressive.

### Four Words That Shape the Voice

- **Warm** — the reader should feel held, not handled.
- **Gentle** — nothing sharp, nothing clever at the reader's expense.
- **Honest** — we say real things, softly. Not hard truths delivered bluntly.
- **Unhurried** — the writing breathes. It doesn't rush to a point or a sale.

### How the Voice Actually Reads

Sentences are calm and considered. They don't try to be memorable — they try to be true. A paragraph might sit with a feeling before offering anything practical. That's intentional.

MindCanopy doesn't tell the reader what they're feeling. It acknowledges that something is happening, and gently makes space for them to name it themselves.

There's no urgency in the writing. No 'limited time' energy. No pressure dressed up as encouragement.

### Anchor Phrase

> **"Get the match right, and the rest tends to follow."**

This is the emotional centre of the brand. It says: therapy is mostly about who you're talking to. You don't need to arrive ready. You just need to be paired with someone who can meet you where you are.

### Things We Never Do

- We don't open with the reader's pain as a hook. We don't lead with 'Are you struggling?' or 'Is anxiety ruining your life?'
- We don't make the reader feel like a problem to be solved. We make them feel like a person being accompanied.
- We don't list features to impress. If we mention something MindCanopy offers, it's because it genuinely helps the person reading.
- We don't use dramatic short sentences for effect. Punchy copy feels clever to write and cold to read.
- We don't use words like: healing journey, holistic, empower, safe space, evidence-based, world-class, certified professionals, transformative.
- We never write anything that could sit on a motivational poster or a pharmaceutical ad.

### Words We Watch Out For

These phrases sound empathetic but read as hollow. Replace them with something more specific and human.

| Avoid | Instead |
|---|---|
| You deserve to heal. | You've been carrying a lot. It's okay to put some of it down. |
| We're here to empower your wellness journey. | Sometimes it helps just to have someone to talk to. That's what we're for. |
| Our certified professionals are ready to help you thrive. | We take time to understand what's actually going on — and pair you with someone who can meet you there. |
| Find your safe space with MindCanopy. | Whatever you share stays between you and your therapist. No one else needs to know. |
| Don't wait — start your healing journey today. | Whenever you're ready, we're here. |
| MindCanopy connects you to a therapist who actually gets it. | Most people who try therapy and stop, stop because the fit wasn't right. We listen to what you actually need, and pair you with someone who can meet you there. |
| No waiting rooms. No commute. No explaining yourself to someone who looks like your uncle. | It's all online — so you can do this from wherever you feel most comfortable, without anyone needing to know. |

## 2. Core Narrative

Therapy is mostly about who you're talking to. The underlying story that runs through every page. Never stated as a list. Always present in the texture.

### The Real Barrier Isn't Awareness — It's Fit

Mental health in India is changing. Slowly, but surely. Conversations that didn't happen are happening now. Most people who might benefit from therapy have at least thought about it.

But there's a quieter problem under the awareness gap. A lot of people who try therapy stop, and when they do, it's usually not because therapy didn't work. It's because the fit wasn't right. The therapist felt distant, or didn't quite get what they were going through, or pushed in a direction that didn't feel like theirs.

MindCanopy is trying to take the friction out of finding the right one. Listening to what someone actually needs — beyond a checklist of issues — and pairing them with a therapist who can meet them there.

### What 'The Right Match' Actually Means

Matching isn't matchmaking. It isn't a quiz that hands you a name. It's the careful work of understanding a person's situation, history, comfort level, what they want from therapy, and what they don't — and then choosing a therapist whose training, temperament, and approach genuinely fit.

When that's done well, the conversation tends to open up faster. The reader doesn't need to be sold on this. They just need to read copy that quietly demonstrates it — the kind of questions we ask, the language we use, the absence of generic promises.

### On Stereotypes — What We Don't Say Directly, But Always Mean

Many people in India have had experiences with advice that felt more like judgement — being told to meditate more, to think of the family, to be grateful for what they have. MindCanopy copy never calls this out. But it gently offers something different.

Our therapists are trained to ask more than they assume. They don't carry a fixed idea of how an Indian person should feel about their life. The reader should sense that they won't be judged by an invisible cultural standard. We never need to say 'stereotype-free'. We just write as if we already are.

### The In-Between Moments

A lot happens in the week between sessions. A conversation that didn't go well. A thought that keeps returning. Something small that suddenly felt heavy. The ability to reach out to your therapist in those moments — not a chatbot, your actual therapist — is something that's hard to explain until you've needed it.

This doesn't need to be sold. It just needs to be described honestly. People will understand why it matters.

> Online therapy isn't a lesser version of something. For many people, it's the version that finally works — when it's the right person on the other side.

## 4. Landing Page — Individuals

For adults navigating anxiety, stress, burnout, anger, loneliness, or loss.

Target keywords: anxiety therapy online · therapy for anxiety · CBT for anxiety · stress management therapy · therapy for anger management · loneliness therapy · online therapy for breakup · depression therapy online

### Research notes — what Indian adults are facing

- Anxiety and depression are the most common presenting issues among Indian adults seeking therapy.
- Burnout, particularly in metro cities, has grown significantly since the pandemic.
- Loneliness — even in crowded homes — is one of the least talked-about but most common experiences.
- Anger is frequently the surface expression of something else: grief, fear, or long-accumulated pressure.
- Many people wait years before reaching out, often not until something external forces the question.

### Hero

Maybe you've been feeling anxious and not entirely sure why.

Maybe you've been short-tempered lately in ways that don't feel like you. Maybe something happened, or maybe nothing happened — it's just been heavy for a while.

You don't need to arrive with a diagnosis or a clear explanation. A lot of people who start therapy don't know exactly what's wrong. They just know that something is, and that they'd like some help figuring it out. That's a completely reasonable place to begin.

### For anxiety

Anxiety can feel different for different people. For some it's a constant low hum of worry. For others it's physical — a tightness in the chest, broken sleep, a mind that won't slow down at night. For some it arrives suddenly, without warning.

Cognitive behavioural therapy is one of the most well-researched approaches to anxiety. It works gently, by helping you notice the patterns in how you think and respond — and slowly building different ones. The therapist we pair you with can walk you through this at a pace that suits you.

### For stress and burnout

Burnout often doesn't announce itself. It creeps in over months — the feeling that rest doesn't really rest you anymore, that work takes three times the energy it used to, that the things you used to enjoy feel distant. By the time most people recognise it, it's been going on for a while.

Therapy isn't about learning to handle more. It's about understanding how you got here, what needs to change, and what a more sustainable version of your life could actually look like.

### For anger

Anger is almost never just anger. Underneath it there's usually something older — hurt that hasn't had anywhere to go, pressure that's built up without release, something that hasn't been said or acknowledged.

Working with a therapist on anger isn't about becoming someone who doesn't feel it. It's about understanding where it comes from, so it stops catching you off guard.

### For loneliness and loss

Loneliness doesn't always look the way people expect it to. You can be surrounded by people — family, colleagues, friends — and still feel completely alone in the things that matter most.

When a relationship ends, the grief is real, even when people around you seem to think it should be over quickly. You're allowed to take the time you need.

### Closing / CTA

There's a free 15-minute call before anything begins — a chance to meet the therapist we've paired you with, ask questions, and see if it feels like the right fit. Nothing starts until you're ready.

## 5. Landing Page — Couples

For partners, married couples, and those navigating something they can't quite name.

Target keywords: couples therapy · marriage counseling online · online couples counselling · couples therapy for communication · does couples therapy work · premarital counselling

### Research notes — what Indian couples face

- Communication breakdown is the most common reason couples seek help — often years after it began.
- Arranged-marriage couples frequently enter therapy after long periods of unspoken conflict.
- In-law dynamics, financial disagreements, and differing expectations are particularly common presenting issues.
- Many couples search online only after other routes — family advice, religious guidance — haven't resolved things.
- Intimacy loss, particularly after children, is rarely spoken about openly but widely searched.

### Hero

Most couples who come to therapy aren't in crisis.

They're two people who care about each other — but who have started to feel like they're speaking different languages, or repeating the same conversations, or drifting apart in ways that are hard to name.

Couples therapy is a space where both of you can say what's actually going on — not the version that comes out in arguments, but the real version underneath. A therapist doesn't take sides. They help you both be heard.

### On communication

When communication breaks down, it's rarely because one person isn't trying. It's usually because both people are trying in ways that aren't quite reaching the other. The same conversation happens again and again, and neither person feels understood at the end of it.

A therapist can help you see what's happening in those moments — not to assign blame, but to give you both something more useful than what you've been doing.

### Does couples therapy actually work?

It can — and it tends to work better when people come before things have been at a breaking point for too long. But there's no right time. Couples come to therapy at all stages, and the conversations that happen there can shift things in ways that years of trying alone sometimes can't.

What it asks of both of you is a willingness to be honest — and a little patience with the process. The therapist you're paired with matters here too: someone whose approach fits the way both of you actually talk and think.

### How it works

1. Either or both of you can book a free 15-minute introductory call.
2. We pair you with a therapist who specialises in relationship and couples work, and whose approach suits both of you.
3. Sessions are 60 minutes, once a week, online — from wherever you both are.
4. Between sessions, both partners can message the therapist separately.

## 6. Landing Page — Adolescents

For teenagers, young adults, and parents looking for support for a child.

Target keywords: child therapy near me · online therapy for kids · mental health services youth · behavioral therapy for kids · CBT for ADHD · anger management therapy children · teen counselling India

### Research notes — what Indian teenagers are facing

- Academic pressure and exam anxiety are the most commonly reported issues among Indian teens.
- Only 41% of young Indians aged 15–24 believe seeking mental health support is helpful (UNICEF 2021). Stigma is significant.
- Social media has introduced a layer of constant comparison that previous generations didn't face.
- ADHD, anxiety, and emotional dysregulation are frequently undiagnosed until they show up as academic or behavioural problems.
- Most families only recognise the mental health struggle once it has affected grades or relationships.

### Part A — For the teenager

You don't have to know what's wrong to talk to someone.

You don't have to have a diagnosis, or a story that sounds serious enough, or the right words for what's been going on. A lot of people who start therapy aren't sure why they're there. They just know that something has felt off, and they wanted somewhere to put it.

Your sessions are yours. What you share with your therapist stays with your therapist. Your parents won't have access to what you say.

#### Some things we hear from teenagers

- "I feel anxious almost all the time and I don't know why."
- "My marks are fine but inside I'm not doing okay."
- "I get angry so quickly and I hate how it comes out."
- "I feel like I can't talk to anyone at home about this."
- "I don't even know what I'm feeling — I just know it's a lot."

### Part B — For the parent

You noticed something.

Maybe a shift in mood that's lasted longer than usual. A withdrawal that felt different from ordinary teenage quiet. Grades that changed, or sleep that did, or a short fuse that wasn't there before.

You may not know exactly what's going on. Your child may not either. But you're here, which means part of you thinks it's worth doing something about — and that instinct is worth trusting.

#### What a therapist offers your child

A therapist gives your teenager someone to talk to who isn't a family member, isn't a classmate, and has no stake in what they decide to share. That distance is often exactly what's needed.

The MindCanopy therapists we pair with adolescents are patient, non-judgmental, and trained to work with young people across a range of things — anxiety, academic stress, anger, ADHD, low mood, and more. Getting the pairing right matters even more with teenagers; we put real care into who you end up with.

Everything is online, so there's no clinic, no commute, and no one outside your household needs to know.

### How it works for families

1. You book a free 15-minute introductory call — your child can join, or you can go first.
2. We pair your child with a therapist suited to their age and what's been going on.
3. Sessions are online, weekly, and fully confidential.
4. You can message the therapist with any concerns — but your child's session content stays private.

### Closing / CTA

The first call is free, and nothing is committed to until your child feels comfortable. It can start as just a conversation.

## 11. Strategic Recommendations

Suggestions that extend beyond the brief — each rooted in a gap in the keyword research, the Indian mental-health context, or how the MindCanopy product is built.

### 01 — Language preference in onboarding

India has 22 official languages, and a meaningful portion of tier-2 city users would feel more comfortable if their therapist speaks Hindi — even if sessions happen in English. The act of being offered the choice builds trust immediately. It's also the most concrete way to demonstrate that matching is taken seriously here.

- **Keywords:** 'online counselling in Hindi', 'therapist who speaks Hindi'
- **Action:** Add a language preference question to all four questionnaires. Surface this in the pairing logic.

### 02 — 'I don't know what's wrong' entry point

Among the highest-volume searches — 'mental health therapy' (4,500/month), 'therapy for mental health' (33,100/month) — a large portion are people in early discovery. They haven't named what they're dealing with. If every landing page is written for someone who already knows, this audience falls through the gap.

- **Keywords:** 'do I need therapy', 'how to know if you need therapy', 'mental health therapy'
- **Action:** Add a 'Not sure where to start?' CTA on the homepage. Route to the General questionnaire with warm, exploratory language.

### 03 — Separate sub-page for college and university students

A 19-year-old in Pune is in a completely different emotional situation from a 15-year-old preparing for board exams. Both sit within the Adolescents segment but need different language and different pairing. College students are newly independent, often far from family, and tend to frame their struggles around identity and uncertainty rather than academic pressure.

- **Keywords:** 'online therapy for college students India', 'student mental health support'
- **Action:** Create a /students sub-page under Adolescents with distinct copy and a modified questionnaire.

### 04 — Premarital counselling as a distinct entry point

'Online counseling marriage' receives roughly 12,100 searches per month, with a 22% upward trend. A meaningful portion of this traffic comes from people preparing for marriage — not yet in conflict. Being present for this search, with gentle, future-oriented copy, builds the brand before any crisis and attracts a segment that is both high-intent and underserved.

- **Keywords:** 'premarital counselling online India', 'pre-marriage therapy India'
- **Action:** Add a premarital option in the Couples questionnaire (already drafted). Consider a /premarital landing page.

### 05 — A blog content engine for informational keywords

Keywords like 'cognitive behavioral therapy for anxiety' (246,000/month) and 'anxiety treatment' (110,000/month) cannot be targeted with a product page — they're informational. A thoughtfully written blog article, in the MindCanopy voice, can capture this traffic organically and lead naturally to a CTA. The therapist blog portal is already built into the product.

- **Keywords:** 'what is CBT', 'how does therapy work', 'types of therapy India', 'anxiety therapy techniques'
- **Action:** Build a structured SEO content calendar. Therapists can write; MindCanopy voice-guides the tone. Target two posts per week.

### 06 — A dedicated privacy page

The fear of being found out is the most significant barrier to therapy in India — more than cost, more than stigma in the abstract. A privacy page that speaks directly and warmly to this concern — without being defensive — could convert a meaningful number of undecided users. A bullet point on the homepage is not enough.

- **Keywords:** 'confidential therapy India', 'private therapist India', 'anonymous online counselling India'
- **Action:** Build a /privacy page. Written in the MindCanopy voice — not legal language. Focused on the emotional reassurance, not the technical policy.

### 07 — Corporate or employer-facing track

Workplace stress and burnout are among the fastest-growing search categories in India. Employers are increasingly open to funding mental health support as a benefit. MindCanopy's subscription model maps cleanly to a per-employee structure, and creates a second revenue stream that doesn't require individual acquisition.
